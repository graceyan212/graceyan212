# Grace Yan

CS (Systems) and Math at Stanford. I build things that ship — websites for real
local businesses, small fine-tuned models that beat frontier models at one narrow
job, and a content engine that runs itself.

### What I'm most proud of

**[website-redesign](https://github.com/graceyan212/website-redesign)** — Redesigns
for local businesses: a wedding venue, a pizza place, a coffee shop, a running
store. Hand-written HTML and CSS, no framework, no build step. Every fact on every
page is sourced, because a redesign that invents things about someone's business is
worse than no redesign.

**[content-loop](https://github.com/graceyan212/content-loop)** — An autonomous
social-content pipeline: it writes, gates, renders, schedules, reads its own
numbers, and proposes changes to its own defaults. The compliance gate is
deterministic and the A/B math is pure, so the parts that must always hold are
unit-tested rather than left to a model. Built during my internship at Alpha AI
Engineering, where the engine drove 138K+ organic views across 3 brands in 2 weeks.

**[toki](https://github.com/graceyan212/toki)** — Mechanical keyboard sound for
macOS. A menu-bar app in Swift plus its landing page, whose Web Audio demo
reproduces the app's synthesis so you can hear it before downloading anything. It
asks for Input Monitoring rather than Accessibility — a keyboard-sound app that can
read your screen is one nobody should install.

**[bible-slm](https://github.com/graceyan212/bible-slm)** and
**[golf-swing](https://github.com/graceyan212/golf-swing)** — Small fine-tuned
models that beat a frontier model at one specific thing. The Bible one holds its
ground under repeated pushback where a well-prompted base model folds.

**[gmat-speedrun](https://github.com/graceyan212/gmat-speedrun)** +
**[anki](https://github.com/graceyan212/anki)** — A GMAT study app built on a fork
of Anki. Desktop and iPhone share one Rust engine through a C-ABI bridge, so the
scheduling change ships to both rather than being a Python-only add-on.
